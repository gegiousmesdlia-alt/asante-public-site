Our story

Two decades of keeping records straight.

Asante & Grove didn't start as a real estate agency. It started as a filing cabinet.

In 2003, a title clerk named Adaeze Asante got tired of watching good buyers walk away from good houses over paperwork that didn't add up — a lien nobody disclosed, a survey that didn't match the deed, a seller's agent who "forgot" to mention the foundation report. She started keeping her own shadow files: properties she'd personally verified, cross-referenced against county records, clean enough to put her name on.

Word got around. By 2006 she'd left the title office and opened a two-desk practice with a law-school classmate, Marcus Grove, who handled the contracts side. The name was supposed to be temporary. It's been on the door for twenty years.

What hasn't changed

Every listing that carries our name has been checked against its title before it goes live — that rule is older than the company's incorporation paperwork. We'd rather tell a buyer "not yet" than let them find out about a problem after closing.
